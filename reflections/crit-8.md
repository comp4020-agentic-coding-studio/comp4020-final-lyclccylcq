# Crit 8: It's alive!

## 1. What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

My first working version passed every check and still felt wrong. It was five dashed zones with coloured dots, plus a logging panel beside them: a workout tracker with a gym theme. The breakthrough was deciding that the gym itself had to be the interface. Once I redirected the work towards a pixel room where your real workout decides what your character does, the next decisions almost made themselves. You start a set by walking to a machine, not by picking from a list. The setup opens only when you arrive. Other people appear as people on equipment, not as rows in a table.

The same idea then settled persistence. Rather than a profile page, or a history screen at reception, history went into your own locker. The floor shows what is happening now; the locker keeps what you've done. Persistence stopped being a course requirement and became part of the place.

## 2. What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I used to measure progress by what had been added. This week the useful question was narrower: does this change make it feel more like training in a shared gym? Dashboards, leaderboards and coaching were all within reach, and none of them passed that test.

Working with an agent made this concrete. The first build put everyone's weights on the public floor and running totals on the welcome screen. Both worked, and both pulled the app back towards a tracker. I want to be a developer who holds the design intent, writes it down as rules and checks, and treats "it works" as the start of the review, not the end.
